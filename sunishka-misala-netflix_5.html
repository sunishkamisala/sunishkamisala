A few projects that made a difference

The stories behind some of the work I'm most proud of.

The Self-Serve Dashboard

This is probably the project I'm most proud of. We were trying to create one view of how customers interacted with our self-service experience, but the data lived across Adobe Analytics, Google Analytics, and ServiceNow.

A big part of the work was bringing those sources together in Snowflake and figuring out how they mapped to one another. We had to work through the underlying logic, understand exactly what each source was measuring, and make sure metrics that looked similar actually meant the same thing.

I also owned most of the stakeholder relationships end to end, working with 50+ people across internal teams and external partners while the scope continued to evolve. After several months of work, we hit our December deadline and launched with real data.

It became the source of truth for 200+ stakeholders and helped identify opportunities that drove a 40% increase in self-service resolution rates across 8 cross-functional teams.

Finding a new signal for account risk

Support and Usage data had never been part of our end-of-quarter account-health analysis. I wanted to see if support-case behavior could tell us something earlier about which Now Assist customers were struggling to onboard or weren't finding enough value.

Using three quarters of historical data, I segmented accounts across five ACV tiers and analyzed signals including case volume, CSAT, time to resolution, and NLP-clustered case themes. I then modeled those patterns against current accounts to identify where intervention was most urgently needed.

The analysis surfaced $60M in accounts potentially at risk over the following 90 days. Teams used the findings to target onboarding and support interventions, while Sales received account-level context ahead of customer conversations. What started as an ad hoc analysis ultimately helped establish support-case volume as an account-risk signal.

Moving 15+ dashboards off Tableau

I led the migration of 15+ enterprise dashboards from Tableau to Power BI as the primary point of contact, which meant standardizing metric definitions and KPI governance so people could trust the numbers without checking with the analytics team first. It wasn't just a lift and shift. We used it as a chance to fix reporting that had quietly drifted out of alignment.

A data dictionary I built to fix a recurring problem

The team kept running into the same problem: everyone had a slightly different definition of the same KPI. I designed a Claude-powered data dictionary that centralized those definitions and the business logic behind them, and it's now used by 50+ people as the team's source of truth.
